Machine fault detection using vibration signals

A Signals and Systems mini project. We use the Fourier transform and energy/power analysis to tell a healthy machine from one with a bearing fault, using Scilab.

Objective

Machines such as motors and pumps shake in a pattern that changes when a bearing starts to fail. We analyse that vibration signal in the time and frequency domains and decide whether the machine is healthy or faulty.

Course outcome 1

Generate signals, add noise and apply basic signal operations.

Course outcome 3

Analyse spectral content with the Fourier transform.

Experiments 3, 5 and 8

Energy and power, magnitude spectrum, and a self-designed real-signal experiment.

Method

  1. Generate the healthy signal: a shaft-frequency sine, its second harmonic and random noise.
  2. Generate the faulty signal: add periodic impacts at the fault frequency, each ringing at a structural resonance.
  3. Plot both signals in the time domain.
  4. Compute the FFT and plot the magnitude spectrum of each signal.
  5. Compute energy and power in the time domain and check them against the spectrum with Parseval's theorem.
  6. Compare RMS, peak and kurtosis, then classify the machine as healthy or faulty.

Energy and power

Energy is the sum of x² divided by the sampling frequency. Power is energy divided by the duration. A faulty bearing adds impact energy, so its power rises, mostly in the resonance band.
